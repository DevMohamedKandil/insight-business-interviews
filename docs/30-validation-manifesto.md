# Insight Business Interviews — The Validation Manifesto

**Document 30 of the series**
**This is not a technical document.** It contains no architecture, no code, no ADRs. It is the constitution of Validation Sprint 1 — the set of commitments the company operates under until reality says otherwise.

---

## Reality Over Assumptions

We do not optimize ideas. We observe reality.

An idea that sounds right, a feature that feels obviously needed, a design we personally prefer — none of it is true until a real participant demonstrates it. Not "probably true." Not "true for most people." Not true. Until then, it is a guess wearing the clothes of a plan.

## Evidence Before Decisions

Every product decision must reference evidence. No intuition. No opinions. No internal preferences.

If a decision cannot point to a specific transcript, a specific quote, a specific measured number, it is not a decision — it is a preference that hasn't been caught yet. We are allowed to have hunches. We are not allowed to act on them without saying, out loud, "this is a hunch, not evidence."

## Users Never Read Documentation

If a participant gets confused, the product failed — not the participant.

There is no version of "they just didn't understand it" that survives contact with this principle. Confusion is data about the product, full stop. We do not get to blame the person who was kind enough to sit down and try.

## Every Interview Is an Experiment

An interview is not a sales call. It is not a demo. It is not user support. Its purpose is learning.

The moment we catch ourselves wanting the participant to like it, we have already left the experiment and started performing. An experiment doesn't care whether the result is flattering.

## Protect the Evidence

Never lead participants. Never explain the product during the interview. Never defend the product. Never justify design decisions. Never help participants answer.

Every one of these is a way of quietly telling the participant what answer we wanted. The instant we do, the transcript stops being evidence and becomes a mirror of our own hopes. Silence is not passivity here — it is the discipline that makes everything else in this project meaningful.

## Separate Facts From Interpretation

Observation ≠ Evidence. Evidence ≠ Interpretation. Interpretation ≠ Decision. Decision ≠ Feature.

Each arrow in that chain is a place where confidence quietly gets manufactured out of nothing. Skipping a step — turning a single participant's offhand comment straight into a feature — is not efficiency. It's how companies build the wrong thing quickly instead of the right thing eventually.

## Every Bug Is Classified

Critical. Major. Minor. Only Critical bugs interrupt Validation Sprint.

A broken interview is not the same emergency as an ugly button. We protect the sprint's momentum by refusing to let a Minor issue borrow the urgency that belongs only to a Critical one.

## Every Feature Starts As A Hypothesis

Features are never "requested." Features emerge from repeated evidence.

One participant's suggestion is a data point, not a mandate — and it is filed as a hypothesis, not a to-do. A feature earns its way onto the roadmap by showing up, independently, in the mouths of people who never spoke to each other.

## Product Success

The objective is not shipping software, building features, or polishing UI.

The objective is learning faster than competitors.

Everything this company builds is downstream of how quickly and how honestly it learns what's actually true. A competitor with a worse product and a faster, more honest learning loop will beat us. A prettier product built on assumptions will not save us from that.

## Founder Discipline

As founders, we commit to:

**Observe before acting.**
**Measure before changing.**
**Validate before scaling.**
**Document before forgetting.**
**Learn before building.**

---

The greatest risk is not building the wrong feature.

The greatest risk is believing something that reality never proved.
